# The micro/macro lens — required for entries, editorials, and comparisons

A deliberate, negotiated addition to this project's content standard
(2026-09-15). Read this alongside `EDITORIAL_VOICE.md` (editorials) and
`OFFICIAL_SOURCES.md` (sourcing) — this file applies more broadly, to
`scripts/entries/*.ts` content packs and `comparisons` content too, not just
editorials.

## The requirement

Every entry, editorial, and comparison should show its subject at **two
levels**, not just one:

1. **Micro — what changed in an ordinary person's, household's, or small
   business's actual day-to-day life.** Not "GDP grew X%" alone, but what
   that meant for someone: a shorter wait, a bill they no longer pay, a
   trip they no longer have to make, a risk they no longer carry. This is
   the same throughline `EDITORIAL_VOICE.md` already establishes for
   editorials (the ration-card queue, the walk for water, the wait for a
   telephone connection) — now made an explicit requirement for entries and
   comparisons too, not something that only shows up in the editorial
   layer.
2. **Macro — what changed for India's standing, capability, or autonomy in
   the world.** Concretely: self-reliance (does India depend on someone
   else for this now, or less than before?), agenda-setting capacity (can
   India shape an outcome — a G20 declaration, a vaccine-supply decision —
   rather than just receive one?), and reputation (how is India perceived
   or treated differently as a result). Call this the "sovereignty" lens
   if useful shorthand, but see the boundary below — it means *capability
   and autonomy*, not territorial or military claims.

Neither lens overrides the honesty rule already in force everywhere on this
site. If the micro-level effect is genuinely mixed (PM-KISAN's real reach
alongside its real leakage), say so. If the macro-level effect is genuinely
modest (India's arms-export share is still negligible globally even as the
domestic trend grows fast), say so. The lens changes what gets discussed,
never how honestly it gets discussed.

## The boundary — "sovereignty" means capability, not territory

This project already declined a specific request (2026-09-15) to cover
Kashmir, POK, "wars and border disputes," and cross-border terrorism — see
`PROJECT_LOG.md`'s "declined topics" note. That boundary does not move for
this lens. When applying the macro/sovereignty angle:

- **In scope**: self-reliance in defense production, vaccine/pharma
  manufacturing capacity, digital public infrastructure, energy security,
  agenda-setting in multilateral forums (G20, ISA), diplomatic reach
  (evacuation operations), economic scale/rank. All of this is about
  India's own growing capability and autonomy — it doesn't require taking
  a position on a disputed claim or characterizing another country
  adversarially.
- **Out of scope**: anything that requires characterizing a specific
  country as an adversary, adjudicating a territorial dispute, or
  attributing an act (terrorism, conflict) to a named foreign actor. If a
  macro-level angle can't be written without doing one of those things,
  don't force it in — leave the entry's macro dimension thinner rather
  than cross that line.

## How to apply this in practice

- **Entries** (`scripts/entries/*.ts`): when researching/writing a new
  entry, one `bodySections` entry should typically address the micro
  angle explicitly (not just imply it via a stat), and — where a genuine,
  in-scope macro angle exists — another section should address it. Not
  every entry has a strong macro angle (a purely domestic welfare scheme
  might not), and that's fine; don't manufacture one that isn't there.
- **Editorials**: already covered by `EDITORIAL_VOICE.md`'s existing
  voice — this file adds the explicit macro/sovereignty-as-capability
  angle as a second thing to reach for, alongside the historical-hardship
  framing already established there.
- **Comparisons**: the narrative should note, where genuinely applicable,
  what a given international standing (or gap) means both for ordinary
  people (e.g., a lower renewable-generation share can mean higher
  household energy costs or pollution exposure) and for India's global
  capability/agenda-setting position (e.g., a small arms-export share
  limits diplomatic leverage that comes with being a major supplier) —
  again, only where that connection is real and can be stated honestly,
  not manufactured.

## Aggregate numbers need their per-capita partner

A specific, mandatory case of the micro lens, added 2026-09-15 after user
feedback on the "Fourth Largest Economy" editorial: **any time a post
states a big aggregate number — total GDP, total forex reserves, total
defense budget, total exports — it must also state the per-capita or
population-normalized equivalent, with its own source**, not just a vague
gesture at "of course this doesn't mean everyone is rich." "4th-largest
economy" without its per-capita rank in the same paragraph is technically
true and misleading by omission — normalizing by 1.4 billion people is
exactly the arithmetic that turns a macro number into a micro one.

This extends to related base-rate context where it changes what the
aggregate number means for an ordinary person — for example, a narrow
direct-tax base (India: ~6.68% of population filed an income tax return in
FY 2023-24, per the Ministry of Finance's reply to the Lok Sabha) is a
citable fact worth including alongside a GDP-size story, because it's the
same per-capita reality seen from the tax rolls instead of the GDP tables.

**State the fact, not a verdict on the people behind it.** "A narrow tax
base" is a fact. "That portion of the population isn't contributing to the
economy and is just taking subsidies" is a step past what the fact
supports — most people below the tax-free threshold are there because
average income is genuinely low (which the per-capita numbers already
establish), not because they're opting out; they still pay indirect tax
(GST) on nearly everything they buy and are the labor behind the aggregate
number being reported. Cite the base-rate fact plainly; don't editorialize
a moral judgment about the people in it. This is the same discipline as
`EDITORIAL_VOICE.md`'s honesty rule — richer context, not a license to
frame.

## Retrofit note

This requirement is being applied going forward, starting 2026-09-15.
Existing entries/editorials/comparisons written before this date were not
retroactively rewritten as part of adopting it — that would be a separate,
explicit task if the site owner wants it done. New content should follow
this from here on.
