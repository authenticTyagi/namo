# CAG reports and Supreme Court judgments as sources

Two source types, added 2026-09-16, that sit in a genuinely useful spot:
official enough to be `official_primary`, but institutionally independent
of the executive government whose work this site documents — CAG audits
and the judiciary are separate constitutional pillars, not government
self-reporting. That makes them unusually good sources for exactly the
kind of unflattering-but-true finding this site's honesty rule exists
for, since finding problems is literally what an audit or a court case
does.

## CAG reports (Comptroller and Auditor General of India)

- **Tier: `official_primary`.** The CAG is a constitutional authority
  (Article 148), and its reports are laid before Parliament — this is
  about as official as a source gets, and it's specifically an audit
  *of* the government, not government communication.
- **Cite the actual report, not just a paraphrase of it**, wherever
  possible — reports are published at cag.gov.in. Note the report number/
  title and year in `credibilityNotes` (e.g. "CAG Report No. 12 of 2023,
  Ministry of X"), the same way this project already expects for any
  primary document.
- Secondary media reporting on a CAG finding (as already used, e.g. in
  the Beti Bachao Beti Padhao entry) remains fine as `reputable_media`
  corroboration or as the sole source when the report itself is hard to
  access — but reach for the report directly when you can, especially for
  a specific figure or finding this site is going to quote precisely.
- **If the audited ministry/department disputed the finding**, report
  that too, the same way this project already handles the WHO-vs-
  government dispute on COVID excess mortality or the government's
  rebuttal of the parliamentary committee's BBBP ad-spend figure — a CAG
  finding is strong evidence, not automatically the final word if there's
  a documented government response.

## Supreme Court judgments (and, where relevant, High Court)

- **Tier: `official_primary`.** A judgment is an official legal record of
  the judiciary, a body independent of the government being written
  about. Full judgments are available at main.sci.gov.in (or a High
  Court's own site); cite the case name and citation/date in
  `credibilityNotes`.
- **In scope**: rulings on the legality, constitutionality, or
  implementation of a government scheme, law, or policy (e.g. a
  judgment upholding or striking down a scheme, ordering a government
  body to act, or interpreting a law's scope) — this is squarely
  "verifiable government work" in the site's own sense, just examined by
  a different branch.
- **Out of scope, same boundary as everywhere else on this site**:
  a judgment or case that's fundamentally about a specific politician's
  or party's conduct (a corruption case, an election-disqualification
  matter, a defamation suit) rather than about a policy or scheme.
  Citing "the Supreme Court ruled X policy invalid because Y" is in
  scope; citing a court case to characterize a person or party is not,
  for the same reason this project won't take a side on Kashmir or
  attribute an act of violence to a group — it requires adjudicating a
  contested matter this site's method can't referee.
- **Sub-judice matters are not sources.** Cite only a *decided* case, and
  note if it's under appeal/review rather than final — an ongoing case
  isn't a settled fact yet, it's an open question, and shouldn't be used
  to assert something as true.
- Read the actual judgment (or at least its operative holding/summary),
  not just a news headline about it — same "never cite a claim you
  haven't actually read at the source" rule as everywhere else on this
  site. Judgments are long; it's fine to lean on a reputable legal-
  reporting outlet (Live Law, Bar & Bench) to locate and summarize the
  holding, but verify the actual text before citing a specific figure or
  quote from it.
